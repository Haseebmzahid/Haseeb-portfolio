## Architecture and loading

Phones, materials, routing nodes and motion are original procedural code using the existing R3F/Drei infrastructure. Genuine FinFlow screens use UV selection of the unchanged composite capture; mobile uses a clipped image alternative. No GSAP, external animation pack or additional dependency was introduced.

## Architecture and behavior

- Next.js App Router, React, TypeScript, Tailwind and Framer Motion remain the foundation. Native scrolling is retained; no GSAP or competing scroll controller was added.
- `LaptopScene` is dynamically loaded on desktop. It models the aluminium body, thin lid, bezel, hinge, 72 instanced keys including a spacebar, trackpad and ports. - Pointer movement is damped and bounded; entrance and scroll progression preserve screen visibility. Studio lights and a generated Lightformer environment require no remote HDRI.
- `PortraitSequence` combines all three supplied photographs, layered frames, gentle image movement, scroll-driven cuts and explicit manual cut controls. Manual selections take priority until the visitor leaves the sequence. Faces are not generated, morphed or rotated through 360 degrees.

- Mobile/tablet use a screen preview, photographic composition and complete HTML timeline. They do not squeeze orbiting labels into a small viewport.
- Visible animated scenes render continuously because orbiting and damping need frames. Pause/reduced-motion use demand rendering. Scenes unmount offscreen. DPR is bounded at 1–1.5; keyboard geometry is instanced; no postprocessing or external model assets are used.
- Scene boundaries supply fallbacks when WebGL cannot render. Reduced motion and a global pause control are included. Keyboard skip navigation, labelled controls and native summaries remain available.
